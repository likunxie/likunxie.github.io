A Note on AI and Math Conjectures (0ct 16, 2026)

Recently there have been several AI-generated attempts at major mathematical conjectures, including some that I care about, such as the small Cohen–Macaulay conjecture and Serre’s positivity conjecture. We do not yet know whether all of these arguments are correct, and I think they still need serious verification.

I think what makes a conjecture interesting is often the possibility of exploring new mathematical territory, either along the way to a solution or from whatever the solution itself might reveal. A conjecture usually points toward some underlying structure or theory that we are trying to understand. Sometimes a proof reveals that structure clearly, while other times it settles the conjecture without explaining nearly as much. Either way, many questions remain about why the result is true, whether there is a more conceptual explanation, and what else we can learn from it.

A counterexample can leave even more questions behind, since it usually tells us that something fails without revealing much about the underlying structure or why it fails. This makes it even more interesting to understand what causes the failure and under what conditions the conjecture might still hold. Sometimes a counterexample can also point toward new structures or theories that we had not considered before.

So overall, I find the situation quite interesting.
